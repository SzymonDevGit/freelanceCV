Cheltenham Data · Design directions · 5 Aug 2026

Three ways out of the template.

Same content, same palette, same 3D and animation budget. Each direction breaks a different one of the habits that make a page read as machine-made: centred symmetry, uniform rounded cards, even whitespace, and decoration that isn't carrying information.

Direction A

Ledger — the printed commercial report

Everything hangs on a visible twelve-column grid. Hairline rules and leader dots replace cards; figures are tabular and ranged right in a money column. The 3D element is a stack of paper that tips towards your cursor, not a floating glass panel.

Reads as a chartered analyst who sends you a document, not a landing page.

 Direction B

Instrument — the tool, not the brochure

Dense, monospaced and unafraid of small text: a toolbar, a fixed index rail, key/value records and sparklines on every figure. The 3D element pulls one chart apart into its three layers — grid, series, result — and lets you turn the stack.

Reads as someone who builds the dashboards, shown in their own idiom.

 Direction C · chosen

Workshop — sheets on a desk, marked up by hand

Printed sheets propped and overlapping, each a degree off true, with a brass pin holding one down. The accent colour becomes marker pen: a circle round the headline, an arrow to the number that matters, drawn on as you scroll.

Three schemes Every colour, shadow and film is a token, so a palette is one block of variables and nothing else. Switch bottom-right, or deep-link: brown · green · paper.

Direction D · the creative one

The Bench — a steel rule and an evidence board

The same workshop, pushed. A steel rule fixed down the page edge whose scale travels with the scroll and reads off how far you've come — because the job being sold is measurement. The results card becomes a corkboard: the £42,500 pinned at the centre and physically wired with thread to the three facts that produce it, threads behind the slips and pin heads proud of them, so turning the board gives real parallax. Tape over the card corners, a noise-masked rubber stamp on the case sheet.

Reads as evidence assembled by hand — the argument is the layout, not decoration.

 Direction D · on paper · shipped

The Bench, on warm stock — in three pens

D rebuilt light, which is not a variable swap: the rule becomes real steel with dark ticks bitten into bright metal, cork becomes cork, tape darkens the sheet beneath it instead of lightening it, and the shadows warm and shorten because paper under a lamp doesn't cast forty pixels of black. The accent is the pen — three colours a person would plausibly mark a printed sheet with, so the annotation layer stays believable whichever you choose.

Three pens Switch bottom-right, or deep-link: red · ink · green.

Now live The green pen is the site, built out across every section and given a dark theme that follows the visitor's system. This mockup stays as the reference for the other two pens.

Each mockup carries the real copy and the real figures across a header, hero, results deck, figures band, one case study and the footer — enough to judge the direction.
